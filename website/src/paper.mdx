## Abstract

Tables generated by large language models (LLMs) can score well on statistical quality metrics even when individual rows violate hard constraints. Enforcing these constraints during decoding requires checking which candidate tokens still permit a legal completion of the row, and repeated solver queries can make synthesis costly. We propose **TabCD**, an LLM-based **Tab**ular data generator with exact **C**onstraint **D**ecoding. Given a partial row, TabCD computes the set of values for the current field that admit a legal completion and uses this set to mask infeasible tokens before sampling. It also reuses feasible value sets and within-field token checks across decoding steps and rows to uplift generation efficiency. With TabCD, all six tabular generators meet their quotas with zero constraint violations using their existing parameters and vocabularies. TabCD improves the mean of all four statistical quality metrics across 17 datasets and has the shortest synthesis time among the three constraint methods in 14 of the 20 rule configurations.

## Method

The central object is the **residual support**: the exact set of values for a field that still admit a legal completion of the partially generated row. TabCD uses these sets to decide which native tokens can be sampled.

1. **Compile the rules.** Separate independent components and logical branches, and select an exact Box, DBM, Tree, or Table backend within compilation budgets.
2. **Project feasible values.** Condition the legal relation on committed fields and compute the residual support of the current field.
3. **Mask before sampling.** Check full token payloads, including field boundaries. The generator samples retained tokens using its own scores and policy.

Projection reuse shares feasible value sets across compatible rows. Prefix-plan reuse shares within-field token checks. Both preserve the exact mask. Generator-specific bridges connect the same core to **GReaT, PAFT, GraDe, REaLTabFormer, Tabby, and TabuLa**, without changing their parameters or vocabularies.

### Constraint collection

The paper collects rules for **24 datasets** from publisher sources, prior literature, and justified derivations. The main evaluation covers **17 datasets and 20 rule configurations**, including four HFGF versions. The taxonomy distinguishes logical/unary consistency, order coupling, linear coupling, nonconvex coupling, and logical–numerical hybrids.

The scope is **row-level constraints** on declared finite typed domains. Cross-row uniqueness, functional dependencies, and table-wide aggregates are outside the method's scope. Exactness and progress are subject to the compilation and native-representation conditions stated in the paper.

## Results

### Constraint satisfaction

With TabCD, all six evaluated generators deliver their requested quotas with **zero constraint violations**. For the main GReaT comparison, native generation violates rules on 18 of the 20 configurations; TabCD reaches zero violations on all 20.

### Statistical quality and utility

Adding TabCD improves GReaT's macro-averaged **Shape by 1.51%**, **Trend by 3.21%**, **α-precision by 2.47%**, and **β-recall by 7.48%**. The four HFGF configurations are averaged before the mean across 17 datasets is computed.

  <Picture slot="figure" src={qualityFigure} alt="Macro-averaged GReaT quality scores increase by 1.51 percent for Shape, 3.21 percent for Trend, 2.47 percent for alpha-precision, and 7.48 percent for beta-recall with TabCD." />
  <Fragment slot="caption">Statistical quality over 17 datasets. Higher is better; labels show relative gains over native GReaT. Figure 9 from the paper.</Fragment>
</Figure>

Downstream predictive utility varies by dataset. The paper reports both improvements and declines. Nursery and Tic-Tac-Toe are reported separately because their published lookup rules determine the label, so utility gains can reflect supplied rules rather than the generator alone.

### Efficiency and generator adaptability

TabCD is the **fastest constraint method on 14 of 20 configurations** in the GReaT synthesis comparison with DRL and LeJIT. Nursery and US-locations remain costly because their compiled relations contain many branches. This comparison does not claim that TabCD is always faster than native generation.

Across the five additional generators, native macro-averaged CVRs range from 12.2% to 40.7%, while their TabCD variants reach zero. Online-time overhead varies by generator; REaLTabFormer has the largest reported ratio, 14.32×.
